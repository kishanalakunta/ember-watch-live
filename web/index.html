New here? How to read this page · 30 seconds
  1. Colored squares show fire weather: how easily a fire could start and spread. Green is calm, red and dark red are dangerous. Switch Today / Tomorrow / Day 3 above the map.
  2. Small orange dots are places where a satellite saw unusual heat in the last 48 hours. Most are small burns or gas flares; a few are wildfires.
  3. Numbered pins group nearby dots into one possible fire. Red = likely new fire (seen twice, no known fire there). Amber = needs a check by camera, drone or crew. Teal = a fire the fire service already tracks. Grey = likely gas flare.
  4. Click a pin (or a row in the list on the right) to see why it was flagged: what the satellites saw, the weather there, and which towns are downwind.
  5. Click any square to see what drives its fire-weather score: dryness, humidity, wind and days without rain.

This page supports decisions; it never sends crews or warnings by itself. Data refreshes every 30 minutes.
